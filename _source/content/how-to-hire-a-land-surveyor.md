# How to hire a land surveyor in the Middle East and Africa

> **Quick answer:** To hire a land surveyor in the Middle East or Africa, first confirm the firm is licensed by the national or emirate survey authority, then match its specialism (cadastral, topographic, construction or real estate) to your job. Ask for a written scope with the coordinate system, accuracy, deliverables and timeline, and compare at least three quotes.

Hiring the wrong surveyor is expensive. A boundary set out a metre off can put a wall on a neighbour's plot, and a topographic survey in the wrong datum can delay design for weeks. This guide explains how to pick the right firm in any MEA country, what to put in the brief, and how to compare quotes.

#### What type of surveyor do you need?

Most jobs fall into one of five types, and many firms specialise in only one or two.

#### Is the surveyor licensed?

In most MEA countries, only a licensed or registered surveyor can sign a survey that is used for land registration or permits. The licensing body differs by country:

- **UAE:** surveying offices are registered with the land or municipal authority of each emirate. In Dubai, real estate surveyors who prepare unit and plot surveys for title registration work with the Dubai Land Department.
- **Saudi Arabia:** engineering and survey offices are licensed through the professional and municipal systems. Check the firm's commercial registration and professional classification.
- **Nigeria:** surveyors must be registered with the Surveyors Council of Nigeria (SURCON).
- **Kenya:** cadastral surveys must be signed by a surveyor licensed under the Survey Act.
- **South Africa:** land surveyors register with the South African Geomatics Council (SAGC).

Ask for the licence or registration number and check it with the authority. Our [Regulations hub](REGHUB) is adding the rules country by country.

#### What should the brief include?

A clear brief gets you comparable quotes. Include:

1. **Location and size:** plot number or coordinates, area in square metres or hectares.
2. **Purpose:** registration, design, construction, sale, dispute.
3. **Coordinate system and datum:** for example DLTM in Dubai or the national UTM zone. If you are not sure, ask the designer or the authority.
4. **Accuracy:** typically a few centimetres for topographic work and tighter for setting-out.
5. **Deliverables:** CAD drawing (DWG), point cloud, PDF plan, signed certificate, GIS layers.
6. **Timeline and site access:** working hours, permits, escorts.

#### How do you compare quotes?

Compare the scope, not just the price. A cheaper quote often leaves out control points, utility detection, or the signed certificate the authority needs. Ask each firm which equipment they will use (GNSS, total station, drone or laser scanner), how many field days, and who signs the final plan.

#### Red flags

- No licence number, or a reluctance to share it.
- No written scope or deliverables list.
- A price far below the others with no explanation.
- No local coordinate system knowledge.

#### FAQ

**How long does a land survey take?** A single residential plot usually takes one to three days of fieldwork plus office processing. Large sites, corridors or authority approvals take longer.

**Can a drone replace a land surveyor?** A drone can capture topography quickly, but a licensed surveyor still sets control points, checks accuracy and signs the plan.

**Do I need a new survey to sell my property?** It depends on the country and property type. In Dubai, unit and plot surveys for title transactions are prepared by registered real estate surveyors.
