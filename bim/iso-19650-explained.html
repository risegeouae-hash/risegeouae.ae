ISO 19650 explained for Middle East project teams

By GeoAtlas MEA editorial team (BIM management) · Technical review: pending, see editorial policy · Published 19 Nov 2026 · Last reviewed 19 Nov 2026

Quick answer

ISO 19650 is the international standard for managing information over the whole life of a built asset using BIM. It sets out who asks for what information (the client's requirements), how the delivery team plans and produces it (the BIM execution plan and delivery plans), and how it is shared and approved in a common data environment (CDE).

ISO 19650 replaced the UK's PAS 1192 series and is now the reference for BIM contracts across the Gulf and increasingly in Africa. Clients on major projects in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh and Doha often ask for "ISO 19650 compliant" delivery. This guide explains what that means in practice.

The six parts of ISO 19650

Part Title (short) Published What it covers
19650-1 Concepts and principles 2018 Vocabulary, roles and the information management framework
19650-2 Delivery phase of assets 2018 How information is specified, produced and handed over during design and construction
19650-3 Operational phase of assets 2020 Managing asset information once the asset is in use
19650-4 Information exchange 2022 The process and criteria for each information exchange
19650-5 Security-minded approach 2020 Protecting sensitive information about assets
19650-6 Health and safety information 2025 Collecting and sharing health and safety information

Who is who

ISO 19650 replaces familiar job titles with three roles:

  • Appointing party: the client or asset owner who sets the requirements.
  • Lead appointed party: the main consultant or contractor who coordinates a delivery team.
  • Appointed party: each member of the delivery team (architect, structural engineer, MEP, surveyor).

The information requirements

Requirements cascade from the organisation down to each exchange:

  1. OIR (organisational information requirements): what the owner needs to run its business.
  2. AIR (asset information requirements): what it needs to operate and maintain this asset.
  3. PIR (project information requirements): what it needs to make decisions during the project.
  4. EIR (exchange information requirements): what each appointed party must deliver, when and in what form.

The delivery team answers with a BIM execution plan (BEP), a master information delivery plan (MIDP) and a task information delivery plan (TIDP) for each task team. The level of information need sets how much geometry, data and documentation each deliverable needs, replacing loose "LOD" labels.

The common data environment (CDE)

All information lives in a CDE with four states: work in progress (within a task team), shared (for coordination), published (approved for use, such as for construction) and archived (the record). Every file is an information container with a status code and revision, so everyone knows what can be relied on.

How ISO 19650 is used in the region

Dubai Municipality has required BIM on larger and specialist buildings since 2013, through circulars widely reported as No. 196 (2013) and No. 207 (2015), which lowered the thresholds. Updated requirements apply from 1 January 2024. Saudi Arabia's major-project programmes also ask for BIM delivery. Many of these requirements point to ISO 19650 workflows. Verification note: circular numbers and thresholds are taken from secondary sources while we obtain Dubai Municipality's official copies. Check the current requirement with Dubai Municipality before relying on it.

How to get started

  1. Read Parts 1 and 2 first; they cover most project work.
  2. Ask the client for its EIR before you price, and price the BEP and CDE work explicitly.
  3. Agree the CDE platform and naming convention in the first week.
  4. Train the team; vendor academies and training centres in the directory offer ISO 19650 courses.

FAQ

Is ISO 19650 mandatory in the UAE?

ISO 19650 itself is a voluntary standard. It becomes mandatory on a project when the client's contract or the authority's BIM requirements call for it.

What is the difference between ISO 19650 and PAS 1192?

ISO 19650 is the international successor to the UK PAS 1192 series, with similar concepts and updated terms.

Do surveyors need ISO 19650?

Yes, when they deliver survey data or scan-to-BIM models into a project CDE as an appointed party.

Can a company be certified to ISO 19650?

Yes, several certification bodies offer ISO 19650 certification of an organisation's BIM process.
